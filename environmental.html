01 — The Role

Location
Scout

My role was to visit the university campus and capture the physical environment from the standpoint of a student discovering Red Bull Basement for the first time. This was not simply about taking pictures — it was about understanding how a location shapes story, and how the right environment makes a narrative feel personal rather than staged.

Campaign Element — Environmental Research
Discovery Beat
Identifying spaces where a student might naturally pause and notice a poster — unforced, believable entry points into the narrative arc.
Doubt → Action Beat
Library study areas and quieter corners — environments carrying the weight of someone working through a problem without yet knowing the answer.
Confidence Beat
More open, social spaces where the energy shifts — supporting the emotional resolution as the student submits their application.

02 — Theoretical Framework

Space &
Meaning

01
Non-Verbal Communication
Mehrabian (1976) note that environment conveys mood and context before a word is spoken. Every location had to function as silent storytelling, supporting the emotional register of each beat without the audience consciously noticing.
02
Place & Lived Experience
Relph (1976) argued that place holds meaning through lived experience. For a student-focused campaign, locations had to feel organically familiar not like sets. Each space carried a different narrative tension aligned to different story beats.
03
Visual Rhythm & Depth
Barnwell (2004) highlights how production designers consider foreground, midground and background as components that construct visual hierarchy. This framework informed how I documented and recommended each space to the team.

03 — Brand Insight

On the
Ground

Mintel (2026) reports that campaigns directed towardsGen Z perform significantly better when they reflect genuine environments over aspirational or artificial ones. Red Bull Basement positions itself as a platform for real student ideas — so the campus environment needed to reflect that truth. The visit involved noting natural light, footfall patterns, and how each space would read on camera, moving between creative thinking and practical production logic simultaneously.

Sources: Mehrabian (1976) , Relph (1976), Barnwell (2004), Mintel (2026)
