01 — Gibbs' Reflective Cycle

Six
Stages

01
Stage 01
Description
I contributed things such as environmental researcher, creative director and pitch presenter — covering pre-production, scripting and final delivery across project lifecycle. Each role required a different range of skills
02
Stage 02
Feelings
I felt confident in my individual ideas particularly the campus research and script work. There were moments in team communication, however, where I sensed misalignment I did not address quickly enough. That discomfort is important to sit with.
03
Stage 03
Evaluation
The location research and creative direction were strong and appropriate in theory, practically executed, directly useful to the team. Communication with collaborators, and my tendency to assume rather than confirm shared understanding, were clear weaknesses.
04
Stage 04
Analysis
Hargie (2016) notes effective teams over-communicate rather than under-communicate. I operated primarily as specialist and implementer. Belbin (2010) would flag the absence of a monitor-evaluator perspective — stepping back to assess what could be optimised rather than just completing tasks.
05
Stage 05
Conclusion
Confidence in one area can create blind spots in others. Schön (1983) calls this reflection-in-action a skill developed through practice, not assumed. I need to practise stepping back from my role periodically to assess the wider process, even when things feel like they are going well.
06
Stage 06
Action Plan
Structured check-ins. A deliberate habit of questioning what can be optimised. More attentive listening in briefings. Earlier escalation of concerns rather than working around them. Three specific, actionable changes — not aspirational resolutions.

02 — Honest Self-Assessment

What Could
Be Better

The honest version of this reflection is not flattering in places — and that is the point. There is a tendency, particularly when you feel strong in your own contribution, to stop looking critically at the overall process. I was guilty of this. My communication with the team had gaps. I assumed things were understood when they had not been explicitly confirmed, which created small inefficiencies that could have been avoided with more proactive check-ins.

"Confidence in one area can create blind spots in others. The most effective team contributors move between doing and evaluating."

Personal Reflection — Red Bull Basement Campaign 2026

Communication
Assumed alignment rather than confirming it. Hargie (2016) is clear on the cost of this in group contexts — effective teams over-communicate, especially during production phases where timing matters.
Too Comfortable
At times too assured in my own assessment of how things were going. Belbin's (2010) monitor-evaluator role is one I did not occupy enough — too focused on delivering my part, not enough on checking the overall picture.
Role Focus
I functioned primarily as specialist and implementer. The team needed me to also occasionally shift perspective — questioning what could be optimised rather than assuming the plan was sound.

03 — Moving Forward

Action
Plan

01
Structured Check-Ins
Rather than assuming alignment, I will build a habit of brief, scheduled check-ins with collaborators during any team project. A simple habit — but one that meaningfully changes team dynamics and prevents avoidable miscommunication from compounding into bigger problems closer to a deadline.
02
Wider Perspective Practice
During projects I will consciously step back from my own role at regular intervals to assess the bigger picture. Asking "what can be optimised?" rather than "is my part done?" shifts me from specialist to monitor-evaluator — a role my teams need from me, not just a nice-to-have.
03
More Attentive Communication
Active listening in briefings and earlier escalation of concerns rather than working around them. This applies both to communicating outward and to picking up on signals from teammates that something needs addressing. Earlier intervention reduces pressure on everyone and keeps the project on track.
Sources: Gibbs (1988), Hargie (2016), Belbin (2010), Schön (1983)
