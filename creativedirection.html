01 — The Role

Script &
Direction

My role centred on developing and refining the campaign script, and ensuring what was written could be executed on the day. Creative direction at this stage is less about grand vision — it is about translation, taking a structure and working out what it looks, feels and sounds like in practice.

Beat 01
Discovery
A student on campus stumbles across Red Bull Basement. The tone needed to feel light and curious — an accidental encounter, not a sales moment.
Beat 02
Doubt → Action
The student begins thinking through their idea — the internal resistance before something clicks. Zettl (2017) warns against tonal shifts that are not carefully managed; this beat had to carry real weight.
Beat 03
Confidence
The submission. Quiet, earned, not forced. Mintel (2026) notes that Gen Z respond to relatable over aspirational — so the ending had to feel like a real moment, not a triumphant crescendo.

03 — Theory & Brand Lens

Thinking
Behind It

Director's Preparation
Rabiger (2014) statess the director's preparatory phase as understanding the text deeply enough to identify production problems. I annotated the script, flagged transitions that needed tightening, and cross-referenced each scene against the visual concept to ensure nothing drifted from the brief.
Tonal Coherence
Zettl (2017) writes that tonal shifts not carefully managed break audience investment. The opening needed to feel light and curious; the middle had to carry genuine uncertainty; the closing had to feel earned, not forced. I used this framework to evaluate each section of the script.
Brand Alignment
Mintel (2026) notes that energy drink brands targeting students increasingly use aspirational but relatable narratives. The creative direction had to honour this — the student needed to feel like someone real working through a real challenge, not a brand ambassador performing enthusiasm.
Sources: Rabiger (2014), Zettl (2017), Mintel (2026)
